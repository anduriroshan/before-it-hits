Why terrain height decides whether street flooding is believable

Street flooding that matters is 15–60 cm deep. A global 30 m terrain model averages a whole stretch of road into one value and carries height errors measured in metres. A short dip — an underpass, a low junction — can disappear entirely. Drag the rain and switch the terrain source: the same rain gives different water depths on the same street.

Why ranking still works. The error is spatially correlated over distances larger than a city block, so which spot is lowest survives even when how deep does not. That is the whole basis of the screening tier.
DTM, not DSM. Surface models include rooftops and trees. Water runs on bare earth. This is why DeltaDTM is the default and Copernicus GLO-30 is only a flagged fallback — in a surface model, buildings become hills and narrow roads vanish.
What we say on screen, every time. “30 m terrain screening, not a flood forecast. Relative ranking only.” Never a centimetre figure from our own model — only from the official KSDMA layer, which is not our output.
